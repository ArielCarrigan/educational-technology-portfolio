Welcome to my Screencast portfolio! This section highlights my experience creating instructional videos using both traditional screencasting methods and artificial intelligence.

Traditional Screencast

IXL Student Guide

I created an instructional screencast to help my sixth-grade math students navigate IXL and understand their weekly learning expectations.

Using screen recordings, screenshots, narration, visual cues, and Microsoft Clipchamp, I demonstrated how students can access assigned skills, complete their diagnostic activities, and work through their personalized action plans.

Reflection: Educational Value of AI in Video Development

Creating an educational video using Pictory AI allowed me to explore the potential of artificial intelligence as a tool for instructional video development. One of the greatest educational benefits of Pictory is its ability to transform a written script into a narrated video with accompanying visuals. This provides educators with an opportunity to create instructional resources without needing extensive experience in video editing, animation, or audio production.

As a virtual middle school math teacher, I can see several ways this technology could support student learning. Educators could create short instructional videos to introduce new concepts, review previously taught material, or provide additional support for students who need reinforcement. Combining narration with relevant visuals can help students better understand information, particularly when learning independently in an online environment. I also appreciated the ability to incorporate my previously created solar eclipse animation, demonstrating how AI-generated content from different applications can be combined into a single educational resource.

Although Pictory offers several advantages, my experience also revealed limitations. AI-generated visuals do not always accurately represent the intended content, requiring educators to carefully review and revise their videos. Additionally, subscription costs and generation credits can limit opportunities for experimentation and revision. While AI has the potential to save time, the process of learning a new platform, selecting appropriate visuals, and making adjustments can still require considerable effort.

Overall, I believe Pictory AI has educational value when used as a tool to support, rather than replace, thoughtful instructional design. Educators must remain responsible for ensuring that the content is accurate, relevant, and aligned with learning objectives. This experience helped me recognize how artificial intelligence can expand the possibilities for educational video development while reinforcing the importance of human judgment, creativity, and instructional expertise.
